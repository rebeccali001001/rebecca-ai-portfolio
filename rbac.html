MODULE 06 · AUTHENTICATION & SECURITY

RBAC

Role-based access control assigns permissions to roles, then assigns roles to users or services.

01 · What is it?

Role-based access control, or RBAC, is an authorization model that controls access through roles instead of assigning every permission directly to every user.

A role represents a job or responsibility, such as support agent, manager, or administrator. Each role has a defined set of permissions.

02 · Think of it like...

Think of RBAC like access badges in an office building.

People receive badges based on their job. A support badge opens support areas, while an administrator badge opens additional areas.

The building manager changes the access rules for a role instead of editing every badge one by one.

03 · What does it do?

RBAC keeps authorization rules organized and makes access easier to review as an application grows.

Group Permissions

Collect related permissions into a named role.

Assign Responsibilities

Give users or services roles that match their work.

Reduce Repetition

Change one role definition instead of many user records.

Support Review

Make it easier to see who can perform sensitive actions.

04 · Where is it used?

RBAC is common in applications where different people or services need different levels of access.

Business ApplicationsTeams and departments

Separate access for employees, managers, and administrators.

Admin ConsolesOperational controls

Limit sensitive settings and maintenance actions.

Developer PlatformsProjects and environments

Control who can view, deploy, or change resources.

Service AccountsMachine access

Give an automated service only the permissions it needs.

05 · Example

Role: support_agent
Permissions:
- ticket.read
- ticket.update

User: Maya
Assigned role: support_agent

When Maya tries to update a customer ticket, the system checks her role, finds the matching permission, and allows the action. The same role can be assigned to other support agents.

The role is not the user and the permission is not the resource. RBAC connects them through an authorization decision.

06 · How it connects

Authentication→User→Role→Permission→Authorization Decision

Authentication identifies the caller. RBAC helps authorization decide whether that caller may perform a specific action.

07 · Do Not Confuse

RBAC

Groups permissions into roles and assigns those roles to users or services.

Authentication

Checks who or what is making a request. It does not by itself decide what the caller may do.

Permission

Describes an allowed action or access rule. A role can contain many permissions.

RBAC is an authorization design. It normally works after identity has been established.

09 · What to Remember

RBAC controls access by assigning users or services to roles and assigning permissions to those roles.

Good role design keeps access understandable, reviewable, and limited to what each responsibility requires.
