What is RAG?

Retrieval-Augmented Generation gives a model relevant evidence from an external knowledge source before it answers.

Definition

A RAG system prepares source material, retrieves relevant passages for a question, and supplies those passages as context to a language model before generation.

In plain language

RAG is the difference between asking a person to answer from memory and letting them open the right policy page first.

Key points

  • Retrieve first: search the knowledge source before drafting.
  • Context is selective: too much text adds noise and cost.
  • Trace the source: preserve document IDs, versions, and citations.

Sample: a current settlement policy

An operations assistant is asked whether an exception applies. It retrieves the current policy passage, places it beside the question, drafts an answer, and cites the section instead of relying on an old memory.

01 · PrepareChunk policy pagesMake source text searchable.
02 · RetrieveFind matching passagesUse the question as the query.
03 · GenerateDraft with contextThe model sees selected evidence.
04 · CiteShow the sourceA reviewer can inspect it.

Where it is useful

Internal knowledge assistants, support, policy lookup, document Q&A, onboarding, and operational workflows where facts change faster than model training.

Practical caution

RAG cannot fix a missing, stale, inaccessible, or poorly chunked source. Retrieval quality needs its own tests.
