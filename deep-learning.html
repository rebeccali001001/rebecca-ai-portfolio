What is Deep Learning?

Deep Learning is machine learning built with many neural-network layers that learn increasingly useful representations from data.

Definition

Each layer transforms the input. Early layers can notice simple signals; later layers combine them into richer patterns for a task.

In plain language

Think of a stack of kitchen sieves: each layer filters and combines what came before until the whole object or intent becomes recognizable.

Key points

  • Representations: layers learn useful internal features.
  • Hierarchy: simple signals can build into shapes, objects, or intent.
  • Capacity: more layers can capture richer patterns.
  • Trade-off: data, compute, and evaluation become more important.

Sample · a stack of kitchen sieves

Imagine sorting ingredients through several sieves. The first catches simple pieces; later sieves combine those pieces until the helper recognizes the complete ingredient or request.

Layer 1Simple signalsEdges, colors, or short sounds stand out.
Layer 2Combine partsSimple signals become shapes or sound patterns.
Layer 3Recognize wholeParts become a tomato, word, object, or intent.
OutputSupport a taskThe representation feeds a classification or generation decision.

Where it is used

Computer vision, speech recognition, translation, image generation, medical imaging support, robotics, and modern language models.

Key takeaway

Deep learning learns increasingly useful representations through many connected layers.
