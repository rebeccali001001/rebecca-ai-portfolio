What Are AI Guardrails?

Guardrails are controls designed to reduce unsafe, unwanted, or invalid AI behavior.

They can check inputs, restrict actions, validate outputs, or require additional approval.

Guardrails can exist at multiple points in an AI system.

Types of guardrails

Input controls

Check or filter incoming content.

Example: block an unsafe or manipulative request.

Output controls

Validate generated results.

Example: reject an answer that contains unsupported claims.

Tool controls

Limit the actions an agent can call.

Example: allow search but not deletion.

Permission controls

Restrict access to systems and data.

Example: deny payment release.

Human approval

Require review for sensitive actions.

Example: approve a large refund.

Guardrails and related controls

Guardrail vs Guarantee

Guardrail: reduces risk.

Guarantee: would mean failure cannot happen.

Guardrails improve safety, but they do not make an AI system infallible.

Guardrail vs Permission

Guardrail: a broader control concept.

Permission: a specific rule about what an action or resource allows.

Example: guardrail: high-value payment requires control; permission: agent cannot release payment.

Guardrail vs Validation

Validation: checks whether something meets defined rules.

Guardrail: broader system control that may include validation, permissions, filters, or approval gates.

Guardrail vs Human approval

Guardrail: can be automated or human-controlled.

Human approval: a person explicitly reviews an action.

Human approval can be one type of guardrail.
