What is grounding?

Grounding connects a model’s response to trusted, task-relevant evidence so a person can inspect where the answer came from.

Definition

A grounded workflow retrieves evidence, places it clearly in context, tells the model how to stay within that evidence, and preserves citations or source IDs with the answer.

In plain language

Grounding is giving the answer a receipt: the source may still be wrong or incomplete, but the claim is inspectable.

Key points

  • Trusted sources: define which documents and versions are approved.
  • Evidence boundary: answer from retrieved context or say what is missing.
  • Traceability: keep citations, timestamps, and confidence signals.

Sample: an exception with a receipt

An operations assistant answers a settlement-exception question from the current policy excerpt, cites section 3.1, and declines to confirm when no approved source is retrieved.

EvidencePolicy v4.2 · §3.1The approved source.
AnswerException applies after approvalA bounded claim.
TraceSource ID + timestampA reviewer can inspect it.
Fallback“I can’t confirm”No evidence means no guess.

Where it is useful

Policy assistants, regulated operations, customer support, audit-sensitive summaries, and any workflow where a fluent but unsupported answer is risky.

Practical caution

Grounding reduces unsupported claims; it does not make stale, incomplete, or misunderstood evidence automatically correct.
