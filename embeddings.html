05 · Embeddings, RAG & Vector Search

What are embeddings?

Embeddings turn content into coordinates that preserve useful relationships, so a system can search by meaning instead of only by exact words.

Definition

An embedding model maps text, images, or other content to a list of numbers called a vector. Similar inputs tend to land closer together in that vector space.

In plain language

An embedding is like putting a sentence on a map of meaning. “The ship has sailed” can land near “after departure” even when the words are different.

Key points

  • Representation: a vector helps a machine compare meaning.
  • Not an answer: it helps find evidence; another step must use it.
  • Quality matters: model choice, chunking, metadata, and language affect retrieval.

Sample: the policy phrase that never says “sails”

A customer asks, “Can I cancel after the vessel sails?” The question and policy chunks are embedded separately. The closest chunk discusses “post-departure cancellation,” so the system can find the right passage without an exact keyword match.

Vector[0.21, −0.08, 0.74…]Meaning as coordinates.

Where it is useful

Semantic search, help-centre retrieval, duplicate detection, recommendation, document clustering, and multimodal discovery.

Practical caution

A close vector is a candidate, not proof. Keep source permissions, freshness, filters, and citation checks in the retrieval workflow.
