What is Chunking?

Chunking splits large documents or data into smaller pieces that are easier to search and retrieve.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Chunking divides content into smaller units called chunks.

Chunks may be paragraphs, sections, fixed token ranges, or semantic blocks. The purpose is to make retrieval more manageable and relevant.

Think of it like...

Think of cutting a large textbook into index cards.

When someone asks a question, you can retrieve only the cards that contain the useful section instead of carrying the whole textbook into the answer.

How it works

1 · DocumentStart with contentA document or other source is ready to prepare.
2 · StrategyChoose boundariesThe system chooses fixed, structural, or semantic boundaries.
3 · SplitCreate chunksThe content is divided into smaller retrievable pieces.
4 · MetadataKeep useful contextTitles, source names, positions, or other metadata may be attached.
5 · IndexPrepare for searchChunks can be embedded and added to a search index.
6 · RetrieveFind relevant piecesA search system returns chunks related to the user's question.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Textbook

Input: a long textbook

System: splits it by useful sections

Output: a few relevant sections for a question

Business example · Policy search

Input: a company handbook

System: chunks policies with titles and page metadata

Output: the relevant policy passage for an employee question

What it is NOT

Chunk ≠ Token

Chunk: a larger retrieval unit such as a paragraph or section.

Token: a model-readable text unit.

Chunking ≠ Embedding

Chunking: divides content.

Embedding: represents content as numbers for comparison.

Chunking ≠ Summarization

Chunking: preserves pieces of the source.

Summarization: rewrites content in a shorter form.
