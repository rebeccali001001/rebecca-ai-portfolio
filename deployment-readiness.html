What is Deployment Readiness?

Deployment readiness means an AI system is prepared for real users and real operating conditions.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Deployment readiness is the evidence-based state of being ready to operate an AI system in its intended environment.

It includes model quality, functional tests, safety controls, monitoring, failure handling, access, cost, latency, ownership, and rollback plans.

Think of it like...

Think of it like opening a new shop.

A shop needs working equipment, trained staff, safety procedures, supplies, and a plan for problems. Deployment readiness checks the same kinds of conditions before real users depend on an AI system.

How it works

1 · DefineSet release criteriaAgree on quality, safety, and operating targets.
2 · ValidateRun evaluationsTest common, difficult, and risky cases.
3 · OperateCheck the systemConfirm monitoring, access, cost, and latency.
4 · PreparePlan failure responseDefine owners, fallbacks, and rollback steps.
5 · ReleaseStart with controlsDeploy gradually and review live evidence.

Real-world examples

Business example · Support assistant

Input: a tested support workflow

System: has policy grounding, escalation, monitoring, and owners

Output: a controlled pilot release

AI product example · Document extraction

Input: documents with known fields

System: validates outputs and routes exceptions to review

Output: a measurable production process

What it is NOT

Deployment Readiness ≠ Model Quality

Deployment readiness: covers the complete operating system.

Model quality: is one part of that evidence.

Deployment Readiness ≠ A Demo

Deployment readiness: requires repeatable evidence and controls.

A demo: shows a limited successful path.

Deployment Readiness ≠ No Risk

Deployment readiness: makes risks known and manageable.

No risk: is not a realistic release condition.
