MODULE 07 · SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE

High Availability

High availability minimizes downtime using redundancy, health checks, failover, and multiple instances.

01 · What is it?

High availability means a service is designed to stay usable when an instance, machine, connection, or other component fails.

The goal is not to promise that nothing will ever go wrong. The goal is to reduce the chance and duration of an outage by keeping another healthy path ready, detecting trouble quickly, and moving work to that path.

Core idea: keep the service available even when part of the system is unavailable.

02 · Think of it like...

Think of a shop with several checkout lanes and a supervisor who closes a broken lane and directs customers to open lanes.

Customers keep using the same shop entrance. They do not need to know which lane handles the purchase. The supervisor needs a way to notice that a lane is unhealthy and a spare lane must already be ready to help.

03 · What does it do?

High availability reduces the blast radius of failures and shortens the time between failure and recovery.

Redundancy

Keep two or more instances, paths, or replicas so one failure does not remove the only way to serve users.

Health checks

Test whether an instance is actually ready to receive work, not merely whether its process is running.

Failover

Move traffic or responsibility to a healthy replacement when the current component cannot serve reliably.

Reduced outage time

Detect, isolate, and recover from common failures without waiting for a human to repair the first component.

04 · Where is it used?

Use high-availability patterns where interruption would block an important user journey or business operation.

Customer-facing APIs

Run multiple application instances so one host or process failure does not take down the public API.

Checkout and payments

Keep ordering available during campaigns, while making sure payment and order state remain safe and consistent.

Authentication

Protect login and session-related paths because an unavailable identity service can make the rest of a product feel unavailable.

Databases and queues

Use replicas, automatic promotion, or multiple consumers when the data or work-processing layer also needs a recovery path.

05 · Example

An online retailer is running a one-day promotion. The product team wants shoppers to keep browsing and placing orders if one application machine fails.

  1. The team runs three checkout instances across more than one availability zone behind a load balancer.
  2. Health checks call a readiness endpoint that verifies the instance can reach the dependencies it needs for checkout.
  3. When Checkout 2 stops responding correctly, the load balancer marks it unhealthy and sends new requests to Checkout 1 or 3.
  4. The order database has its own replication and failover plan; adding application instances alone would not protect it.

PM Perspective

Define the user journey that must remain available, the acceptable interruption, and the failure scenarios in scope. Track availability, failed requests, health-check time, failover time, duplicate orders, and recovery communication—not only the number of instances.

High availability is a system property: every critical dependency needs an appropriate recovery path, not just the web tier.

A high-availability design must connect capacity, routing, state, detection, and recovery into one operating plan.

07 · Do Not Confuse

High Availability

Keep a service usable when a component fails by using redundancy, detection, and failover.

Fault Tolerance

Continue operating through a defined failure, often with little or no visible interruption. High availability may briefly interrupt service while it detects and fails over.

Disaster Recovery

Restore service after a larger incident such as a regional outage, major data loss, or destructive event.

Scalability

Handle more demand by changing capacity. More instances can help scale, but they do not automatically create failover.

Backup

Keep a recoverable copy of data. A backup helps restore data but does not serve live requests during an outage.

Load Balancing

Distribute requests across targets. It supports high availability when it has health checks and healthy alternatives to route to.

Redundancy is necessary but not sufficient: without health checks, failover logic, and a protected data path, extra instances can still fail together.

09 · What to Remember

High availability minimizes downtime with redundancy, health checks, failover, and multiple instances.

For a PM, availability is a user journey and dependency question: decide what must stay usable, how much interruption is acceptable, and which failures are in scope.

Multiple instances are only one part of the design. The router, stateful dependencies, data replication, monitoring, and recovery process must also avoid a shared single point of failure.
