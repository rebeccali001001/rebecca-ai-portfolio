What Is Training Data?

Training data is the information a model learns from during training. The examples, text, images, labels, feedback, or other data used during training shape what patterns the model can learn.

What is it?

Training data provides examples. Training changes the model.

01Training dataExamples the model can learn from.
02Training processThe model compares predictions with examples.
03Parameters changeInternal values adjust to capture patterns.
04Trained modelThe model behaves differently on new inputs.

Think of it like...

Learning from many examples.

A student studies books, examples, practice questions, and feedback. Over time, the student develops knowledge and patterns.

Study material→ Training data
Learning process→ Training
What the student learns→ Model parameters and behavior

This analogy is for understanding only. Real model training is mathematical and much more complex.

Why data quality matters

QualityUseful examplesClear, accurate signals.
CoverageEnough varietyImportant cases are represented.
BalanceFair representationOne group or pattern does not dominate.
Noise / biasLimits and distortionsProblems can affect what the model learns.

Poor, incomplete, biased, or low-quality data can affect model behavior. Better data does not automatically guarantee a perfect model.

Key distinctions

Training Data vs Prompt

Training data: used during training.

Prompt: supplied when using a trained model.

Training data helps shape model parameters; prompts guide a current run.

Training Data vs Context

Training data: used to learn.

Context: information available during inference.

Training Data vs Fine-tuning Data

Training data: the broad category.

Fine-tuning data: data used specifically for additional model adaptation.
