03 · Tokens, Context & Inference

What is a Token?

A token is a small unit of text that an AI model reads or generates. It can be a whole word, part of a word, punctuation, or a space.

Topic detail

Language models turn text into token IDs before the Transformer processes it. The tokenizer is model-specific, so a short word may be one token while a rare word, code fragment, or emoji may split into several.

Token count is a measurement of the model's text representation—not a count of ideas or a direct measure of intelligence.

Why it matters

Tokens determine how much input and output fit inside a context budget. They also affect prompt processing work and are commonly used for usage or cost accounting.

request budget = instructions + history + retrieved text + task + output

Sample

For teaching purposes, this sentence can be shown as three simple pieces. A real tokenizer may split it differently.

Easy analogy

Like letter tiles on a table, tokens are the small pieces from which a model assembles and predicts a sequence. The analogy helps with scale, but tokens are learned subword units—not physical letters.
