What is Deployment Readiness?

Deployment readiness means a workflow has enough evidence, safeguards, ownership, and fallback to launch safely.

What is it?

Deployment readiness means a workflow has enough evidence, safeguards, ownership, and fallback to launch safely.

A promising demo is not enough. Readiness includes representative quality, failure recovery, latency, cost, monitoring, permissions, support, and a rollback or human path.

Think of it like...

Think of opening a restaurant. A good tasting dish is only one part; the kitchen, staff, safety checks, supply chain, and response plan must also work.

How it works

1 · ScopeDefine the workflowState the task, users, boundaries, and success bar.
2 · TestRun representative casesInclude normal, edge, and failure scenarios.
3 · SafeguardAdd controlsValidate outputs, permissions, monitoring, and human review.
4 · OperateAssign ownershipDocument support, alerts, cost, latency, and rollback.
5 · LaunchStart with evidenceUse a controlled rollout and keep a fallback path.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Shadow mode

Input: a new assistant suggestion

AI / System: runs without changing the real record

Output: evidence for a controlled launch

Business example · Settlement workflow

Input: a recommendation that affects money

AI / System: requires approval and traceable evidence

Output: a guarded rollout with rollback

What it is NOT

Deployment Readiness ≠ Demo quality

Readiness: evidence and safeguards for a real workflow.

Demo quality: a limited demonstration.

Deployment Readiness ≠ Model accuracy alone

Readiness: quality plus operations and ownership.

Model accuracy: one part of the evidence.

Ready ≠ Risk-free

Ready: meets a defined launch bar.

Risk-free: not a realistic guarantee for changing systems.
