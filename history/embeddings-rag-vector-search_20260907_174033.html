One evidence pipeline, in one picture

These four ideas work together: represent meaning, find the right evidence, put it in the model’s context, and keep the answer tied to a source a person can inspect.

One everyday sample

A library card for a shipment policy

Imagine an operations assistant answering, “What do I need for a delayed shipment claim?” It behaves like a very careful librarian: it turns the question and policy pages into a shared map, finds the closest cards, puts the best card on the desk, and answers only from what the card actually says.

01 · EmbeddingsMake meaning locatableThe question and each policy chunk become points in a meaning map.
02 · Vector SearchFind nearby cardsThe index returns the most relevant claim-document passages.
03 · RAGPut evidence in contextThe selected passages travel with the question into the model prompt.
04 · GroundingAnswer with a receiptThe response cites policy version and says when the evidence is missing.

Conclusion: retrieval can make an answer more useful, but source quality, freshness, permissions, and citations still decide whether it is trustworthy.
