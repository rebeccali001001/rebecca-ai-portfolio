What is Retrieval?

Retrieval is the process of finding relevant information from a collection of documents, records, or other data.

Start with a question“What information would help me answer this?”
Learn the ideaDefinitionAnalogyWorkflowExamplesBoundaries
01 · The core idea

What is it?

Retrieval finds information that may help answer a question.

The information can come from many knowledge sources—not only a search box.

DocumentsDatabasesSearch indexesVector storesAPIs
Important boundary

Retrieval itself does not automatically write the final answer.

02 · A useful mental model

Think of it like...

Think of retrieval like asking a librarian for the most useful books before answering a question.

01You ask a question“What is our travel reimbursement limit?”
02The librarian searchesThey look across the collection for likely sources.
03You receive the best sourcesThe librarian finds the sources, not the final answer.

04 · See it in action

Real-world examples

Company policy

Retrieval: Finds the latest travel policy section.

Output: Relevant policy text.

Product search

“Show me waterproof running shoes.”

Retrieval: Finds matching products.

Output: Relevant product records.

RAG

“How do I reset my company password?”

LLM: Uses the article to answer.

05 · Keep the boundaries clear

What it is NOT

Retrieval ≠ Generation

RetrievalFinds existing information.

not the same as

GenerationCreates a new response.

Retrieval ≠ Vector Search

RetrievalThe broader process of finding relevant information.

one possible method

Vector SearchOne way to retrieve information.

Retrieval ≠ RAG

RetrievalFinds information.

a larger pattern

RAGCombines retrieval with an LLM to generate an answer.
