What is Deployment Readiness?

Deployment readiness is the evidence-based judgment that an AI system is safe and useful enough for its intended release.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Deployment readiness is a decision supported by evidence about quality, safety, operations, and ownership.

Readiness depends on the use case. It may include functional tests, failure coverage, latency, cost, privacy, monitoring, rollback, human review, and a clear owner for incidents.

Think of it like...

Think of it like opening a new shop. Passing the recipe test is not enough: the staff, supplies, opening hours, safety checks, and plan for complaints must also be ready.

How it works

1 · ScopeDefine the releaseName users, tasks, limits, and unacceptable outcomes.
2 · TestCollect behavior evidenceRun functional and failure tests on representative cases.
3 · MeasureCheck operationsMeasure speed, cost, availability, privacy, and observability.
4 · ReviewDecide with ownersPeople with responsibility review risk and mitigations.
5 · ReleaseLaunch with safeguardsUse monitoring, rollback, support, and a learning plan.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Personal assistant

Input: A user's calendar request

System: Handles ambiguity and asks before changing events

Output: Safe confirmation and a recoverable action

Business example · Support assistant

Input: A production support case

System: Passes tests, has monitoring, and has human escalation

Output: A controlled release decision

What it is NOT

Readiness ≠ Model Quality Alone

Readiness: covers system, workflow, people, and operations

Model quality: is one part of the evidence

Readiness ≠ One Successful Demo

Readiness: uses repeatable and adversarial evidence

One demo: can hide edge cases and operational gaps

Readiness ≠ Permanent Approval

Readiness: is a decision for a scope and release

Permanent approval: ignores drift, new risks, and changing workflows

Related concepts

Functional tests + Failure modes + Operations → Deployment decision
Failure handling and adoption complete the path from a tested feature to a supported service.
