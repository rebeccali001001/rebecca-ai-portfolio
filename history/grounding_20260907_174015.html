05 · Embeddings, RAG & Vector Search

What is grounding?

Grounding connects a model’s response to trusted, task-relevant evidence so the answer can be checked against a source.

Topic detail

A grounded workflow retrieves evidence, places it clearly in the model’s context, and asks the model to answer within that evidence. The application can preserve citations, confidence signals, source IDs, and timestamps.

Grounding is broader than adding a citation after the fact: it includes source selection, trust rules, answer constraints, and a path for missing evidence.

A citation makes an answer inspectable; it does not make it automatically true.

Why it matters

In an enterprise workflow, a plausible answer is not enough. People need to know which policy, record, or approved source supports the claim and when the system should stop instead of guessing.

Grounding makes reliability a product contract that can be evaluated and monitored.

Sample

An operations assistant answers a question about a settlement exception. It passes the current policy excerpt into the prompt, cites policy §3.1, and says it cannot confirm the exception when no approved source is retrieved.

Evidence
Policy v4.2 · §3.1
Answer
Exception applies after approval
Trace
source ID + timestamp

Easy analogy

Imagine a careful colleague answering a policy question. They say, “Here is the paragraph I used—and here is what it does not cover.” That makes the answer easier to review. It still does not prove the policy is current or that the paragraph was interpreted correctly.

Key takeaway

Grounding is an evidence contract. Define trusted sources, selection rules, citations, freshness, and an honest abstain path when the evidence is missing.
