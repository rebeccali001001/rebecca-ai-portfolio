What is Deployment Readiness?

Deployment readiness is the evidence-based decision that an AI workflow is safe and useful enough for its intended rollout.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Deployment readiness means a system has passed the quality, safety, reliability, and operating checks needed for its next release stage.

The evidence should cover representative tasks, important failure modes, latency, cost, permissions, monitoring, ownership, rollback, and human fallback. A promising demo is not enough.

Think of it like...

Think of opening a new restaurant. You do not serve everyone because one dish tasted good once. You check the menu, kitchen, staff, safety process, peak-time capacity, and what happens when an ingredient is missing.

How it works

1 · CapabilityTest the main jobRepresentative tasks meet the quality bar.
2 · ReliabilityTest failure pathsErrors are detected and recoverable.
3 · OperationsCheck the runtimeMeasure latency, cost, capacity, and monitoring.
4 · OwnershipAssign responsibilityPeople know who reviews, supports, and fixes it.
5 · RolloutChoose the next stagePilot, shadow, expand, pause, or roll back.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Personal assistant

Input: A reminder workflow in a small pilot.

System: Tests missing dates, duplicates, and confirmation.

Output: A controlled rollout decision.

Business example · Settlement assistant

Input: Representative settlement cases.

System: Runs scorecards, logs evidence, and keeps approval in the loop.

Output: Shadow mode, launch, or a clear blocker list.

What it is NOT

Readiness ≠ A Good Demo

Readiness: Evidence across normal, edge, and operational cases.

Demo: May show only a curated happy path.

Readiness ≠ Perfect Accuracy

Readiness: Defines acceptable risk and safe recovery.

Perfect accuracy: Is not a realistic requirement for every workflow.

Readiness ≠ Deployment Itself

Readiness: A decision and set of evidence.

Deployment: The rollout and operation that follow.

Related concepts

Functional Tests + Failure Modes + Operations → Readiness decision
Evaluation supplies evidence. Adoption and workflow ownership determine whether a technically ready system helps in practice.
