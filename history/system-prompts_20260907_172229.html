04 · Prompting & System Design

System prompts are the operating brief for an AI feature.

They set the assistant’s role, priorities, boundaries, tool rules, and what to do when the request is unclear. They shape behavior, but they do not replace application code, validation, or access control.

Topic Detail

A system prompt is instruction context supplied by the application, usually before the user’s message. A useful one states the job, defines important terms, gives decision rules, names forbidden actions, and specifies when to ask, abstain, or hand off.

A practical recipe

RoleWhat job is being performed?
RulesWhat must or must not happen?
ExitWhen should the system stop or escalate?

Why it matters

Without a clear brief, a model can sound helpful while making inconsistent decisions. A well-scoped prompt makes behavior easier to test and gives engineers a stable place to explain the product contract.

Prompt instructions guide the model; permissions, validators, and business logic enforce the boundary.

Sample

For a support assistant that drafts replies about account access:

You are a support-drafting assistant.
Use only the policy excerpts provided by the application.
Never claim an account change was completed.
If the policy is missing or ambiguous, say what is missing and route to a human.
Return a draft plus: evidence_used, confidence, needs_review.

The sample makes the job and the safe failure path explicit. The application still needs to check permissions, evidence, and the returned fields.

Easy Analogy

A system prompt is like a job brief handed to a new front-desk teammate.

It explains the role, the house rules, and when to call a supervisor. The brief helps the teammate make consistent choices, but it does not unlock doors or approve refunds by itself.

Key takeaway

A system prompt is an explicit behavior brief: useful for steering a model, never a substitute for validation, permissions, or deterministic business rules.
