Adoption

Make the new workflow easy to trust and easy to use.

Definition

Adoption is the people side of deployment: user roles, training, rollout steps, feedback loops, ownership, support, and the incentives around a new AI workflow.

In plain English

AI is a teammate, not a magic button. People need to know what it can do, when to check it, how to correct it, and who owns the final decision.

Key points

  • Design for a role: show the user how the feature changes their day.
  • Make trust visible: expose evidence, uncertainty, and review steps.
  • Roll out gradually: use shadow mode or a small cohort first.
  • Keep feedback alive: assign an owner for fixes and learning.

Application

Use adoption planning for assistants, recommendations, automated triage, and agentic workflows. Training and support are part of the product experience.

A successful rollout is one people choose because it makes work clearer, safer, or faster.

Sample · introduce a new kitchen appliance

Start with one cook, show the safe settings, keep the old method nearby, collect feedback, and expand only when the appliance actually helps. The lunch counter's AI helper should work the same way: clear training, visible review, and a manual fallback.
