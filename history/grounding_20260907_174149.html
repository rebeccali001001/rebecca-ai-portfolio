What is Grounding?

Grounding connects a model's response to trusted, task-relevant evidence that people can inspect.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Grounding is the practice of making an AI response depend on approved evidence and clear source rules.

A grounded workflow selects sources, passes evidence into context, preserves citations or source IDs, and defines what happens when evidence is missing.

Think of it like...

Think of a careful colleague answering a policy question. They show the paragraph they used and say what it does not cover.

How it works

The workflow selects trusted evidence, constrains the answer, and keeps a trace for review.

1 · SourceChoose approved materialDefine trusted documents, records, or tools.
2 · RetrieveFind relevant evidenceSelect current and permitted information.
3 · ContextShow evidence to the modelPlace source text and metadata beside the request.
4 · AnswerStay within the evidenceGenerate a response with citations or limits.
5 · FallbackStop when evidence is missingAsk, hand off, or say what cannot be confirmed.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Policy question

Input: Can I return this item?

System: The assistant uses the current return policy

Output: An answer with the policy section

AI product example · File assistant

Input: A question about a report

System: The product shows the source passage

Output: A traceable response

Business example · Settlement exception

Input: A question about an approval

System: The workflow cites the approved rule

Output: A decision draft or reviewer handoff

What it is NOT

Grounding ≠ Citation After the Fact

Grounding: Connects generation to source selection and limits

Citation After the Fact: Adds a reference after writing

Grounding ≠ RAG

Grounding: An evidence and trust contract

RAG: One workflow that can supply context

Grounding ≠ Truth Guarantee

Grounding: Makes sources inspectable

Truth Guarantee: Does not remove source or interpretation errors

Related concepts

Trusted sources → Retrieval → Context → Cited answer / fallback
Grounding overlaps with RAG but also includes trust, freshness, permissions, and abstention.
