What is Grounding?

Grounding connects an AI response to trusted evidence, data, or rules available for the current task.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Grounding is the practice of giving an AI system reliable external information and requiring the result to stay supported by it.

The evidence may come from retrieved documents, a database, a tool, or a deterministic rule. Grounding can reduce unsupported answers, but it depends on source quality and correct use of the evidence.

Think of it like...

Think of it like a news writer working with a folder of source documents. The writer can draft a clear story, but the claims should remain traceable to the sources.

How it works

1 · SourceChoose trusted informationThe system identifies data, documents, tools, or rules it can rely on.
2 · RetrieveBring relevant evidenceThe current question selects the information needed for the task.
3 · ConstrainSet evidence rulesInstructions define how to cite, abstain, or handle missing support.
4 · GenerateProduce a responseThe model writes an answer using the supplied evidence.
5 · VerifyCheck the claimsThe system or a reviewer checks support, freshness, and permissions.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Product question

Input: “Does this ticket include a checked bag?”

AI / System: uses the current booking record and fare rules

Output: an answer tied to the record

Business example · Policy assistant

Input: a compliance question

AI / System: retrieves approved policy sources

Output: a cited answer or escalation

What it is NOT

Grounding ≠ Fine-tuning

Grounding: supplies evidence at task time

Fine-tuning: changes model parameters through training

Grounding ≠ Citation Only

Grounding: constrains the answer with usable evidence

Citation only: may add a link without checking whether the claim is supported

Grounding ≠ Guaranteed Accuracy

Grounding: improves the evidence connection

Accuracy: still depends on source, retrieval, interpretation, and validation

Related concepts

Trusted source ↓ Retrieval or tool ↓ Evidence in context ↓ Supported answer
