What is Deployment Readiness?

Deployment readiness is the evidence-based judgment that an AI system is safe and useful enough for its intended release.

On this pageWhat is it?Think of it like...How it worksReal-world examplesWhat it is NOTRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Deployment readiness is a decision based on whether an AI system meets defined quality, reliability, safety, operational, and workflow requirements.

Readiness is not a single model score. It includes tested behavior, known failure modes, monitoring, recovery, ownership, permissions, cost, and a fallback when the system cannot complete a task.

Think of it like...

Think of it like opening a restaurant after a test service. The menu may work, but the team also needs safe food handling, staff roles, supply plans, and a response to problems.

How it works

1 · DefineSet release criteriaName the quality, safety, latency, cost, and workflow requirements.
2 · TestRun representative scenariosExercise normal use, edge cases, failures, and recovery paths.
3 · OperatePrepare ownership and monitoringDecide who watches the system and what signals trigger action.
4 · DecideRelease, limit, or stopUse the evidence to choose scope, safeguards, and rollout plan.

Real-world examples

Everyday example · Personal assistant

Input: a reminder workflow

AI / System: passes valid input and handles missing details

Output: a limited release with a clear fallback

Business example · Document processing

Input: a tested invoice workflow

AI / System: meets accuracy, latency, audit, and review requirements

Output: a controlled production rollout

What it is NOT

Deployment Readiness ≠ Model Quality

Deployment Readiness: an end-to-end release decision

Model Quality: one dimension of system behavior

Deployment Readiness ≠ Production Launch

Deployment Readiness: evidence and decision before or during release

Production Launch: the act of making the system available

Deployment Readiness ≠ Zero Risk

Deployment Readiness: known risks have controls and owners

Zero Risk: an unrealistic guarantee for changing systems

Related concepts

Functional tests + Failure modes + Operations + Human ownership = Release decision
