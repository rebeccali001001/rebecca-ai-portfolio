What Is an AI Hallucination?

A hallucination is generated content that sounds plausible but is unsupported, fabricated, or incorrect.

Fluent language can still contain false information.

On this pageDefinitionMain visualThink of it like...Common formsExampleCompareReduce riskRelated conceptsRemember thisVideo

What is it?

Hallucination is fluent but unsupported or incorrect generated content.

A model can produce an answer that sounds confident even when it has no reliable evidence for the claim. Fluent language is a style signal, not proof of truth.

How it happens

Fluent≠Factual

Think of it like...

Someone answers confidently from memory without checking whether the memory is correct.

Confident speaking → fluent output
Wrong memory → unsupported content

AI models do not have human intentions or beliefs.

Common forms

FABRICATED FACTInvented number, date, person, source.
UNSUPPORTED CLAIMNo evidence supports the statement.
WRONG ATTRIBUTIONCorrect-looking fact linked to wrong source.
INVENTED DETAILAdds information that was never supplied.

One simple example

POLICYRefund period: 30 days
USER“What is the refund period?”
BAD OUTPUT“60 days.”Unsupported answer
BETTER SYSTEMRetrieve policy↓Add evidence to context↓Generate answer↓Validate important field

Compare the failure

Hallucination

Incorrect or unsupported generated output.

It is an output failure, not proof of intent.

Deliberate lying

Would imply intentional deception.

AI models do not have human intentions or beliefs.

Uncertainty

The system explicitly signals it is unsure.

Hallucination may sound confident despite being wrong.

Confidence of wording is not evidence strength.
