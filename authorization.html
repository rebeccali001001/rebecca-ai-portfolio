MODULE 06 · AUTHENTICATION & SECURITY

Authorization

Authorization is the process of deciding what an authenticated user or system is allowed to access or do.

01 · What is it?

Authorization evaluates an identity, a requested action, and a resource against access rules.

The result is an access decision: allow, deny, or require additional review.

02 · Think of it like...

Think of authorization like checking which rooms a verified visitor may enter.

The entrance desk may know who the visitor is, but a badge or written rule still determines whether they can enter a particular room.

03 · What does it do?

Authorization turns identity and policy into a decision that protects resources and actions.

Check the Resource

Identify the record, file, page, or service being requested.

Check the Action

Distinguish reading, creating, updating, deleting, or executing.

Apply Policy

Use roles, permissions, ownership, or other access rules.

Enforce the Decision

Allow the request, deny it, or send it for review.

04 · Where is it used?

Authorization is used whenever different identities need different levels of access.

Private DataRecord access

Allow people to see only the records they are permitted to view.

Team ToolsRole-based access

Give members, managers, and administrators different capabilities.

APIsAction scopes

Limit which endpoints or operations a client may call.

High-risk ActionsApproval rules

Require extra review before sensitive changes or transactions.

05 · Example

  1. A signed-in employee requests an invoice.
  2. The system checks the employee’s identity and team.
  3. A policy allows invoice viewing but not deletion.
  4. The system returns the invoice and records the decision.

The same person may be allowed to read one resource, edit another, and access neither a third. Authorization is specific to the action and resource.

Authorization should be checked on the server or trusted enforcement layer, not only hidden in the interface.

Authentication supplies the identity; authorization uses it with context and policy to protect access.

07 · Do Not Confuse

Authorization

Decides what a verified identity may access or do.

Authentication

Verifies who the user or system is.

UI Visibility

Hides or shows controls but cannot replace server-side access enforcement.

A button being visible or hidden is not the authorization decision.

09 · What to Remember

Authorization answers “What may you do?” after the system has an identity to evaluate.

Protect each resource and action with explicit rules, and enforce those rules in a trusted layer.
