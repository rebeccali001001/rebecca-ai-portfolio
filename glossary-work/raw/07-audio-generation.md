## Do Not Confuse Candidates

- Audio Generation ≠ Speech Recognition: audio generation creates new audio; speech recognition interprets existing speech and may turn it into text or meaning.
- Audio Generation ≠ Speech-to-Text: speech-to-text converts spoken audio into written text; it is not itself a new-audio creation task.
- Audio Generation ≠ Audio Understanding: understanding analyzes or interprets existing audio; generation produces new sound.
- Audio Generation ≠ TTS: text-to-speech is one speech-generation use case inside the broader audio-generation category.
- Audio Generation ≠ Music Generation Only: audio generation also includes speech, sound effects, and ambience.
- Music Generation ≠ Audio Generation: music generation focuses on musical audio, while audio generation covers a wider set of sound outputs.
- Speech ≠ Speech Recognition: speech is an audio output or input; recognition is the task of interpreting it.
- Speech Generation ≠ Speech Recognition: speech generation creates spoken audio; recognition extracts text or meaning from spoken audio.
- Sound Effect ≠ Ambient Audio: a sound effect usually represents a specific event or object, while ambient audio represents a surrounding environment or background.
- Ambient Audio ≠ Music: ambience supplies environmental atmosphere and is not necessarily organized as music.
- Voice Reference ≠ Voice Output: a voice reference guides generation; the voice output is the generated result.
- Prompt ≠ Output: a prompt or description guides the model; the output is the resulting audio.
- Condition ≠ Representation: a condition is the supplied guidance; a representation is the encoded or generated information used inside the pipeline.
- Encode ≠ Decode: encoding turns an input into model-usable information; decoding turns a generated representation into playable audio.
- Encoder ≠ Decoder: an encoder represents the input; a decoder reconstructs a waveform or playable signal.
- Audio Representation ≠ Waveform: a representation can be an intermediate model form; a waveform is the signal form used to represent playable sound.
- Waveform ≠ Audio File: a waveform is a signal representation; a file format such as WAV or MP3 is a storage or delivery format, neither of which is named by the page.
- New Sound ≠ Analysis of Existing Audio: producing a new result is different from examining an existing recording.
- Generative AI ≠ Audio Generation: generative AI is the broader category; audio generation is one of its modalities or application areas.
- Audio Modality ≠ Audio Generation: audio is a modality; generation is the operation performed within that modality.
- Style ≠ Mood: style describes musical characteristics; mood describes the emotional quality of the requested result.
- Song ≠ Instrumental Track: a song may include vocals or song structure; an instrumental track is a musical track that does not necessarily contain singing.
- Matching Sound ≠ Exact Recording Copy: the page says the output matches the request or clues, not that it reproduces an original recording exactly.
