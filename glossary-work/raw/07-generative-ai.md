## Do Not Confuse Candidates

- `Generative AI` vs `Artificial Intelligence`: Generative AI is presented as a content-creation category; Artificial Intelligence is the broader field of intelligent computer systems.
- `Generative AI` vs `search engine`: the page contrasts generating a response from learned patterns with retrieving existing pages or records.
- `generated content` vs `existing pages or records`: generated content is produced by the model; existing pages or records are retrieved from elsewhere.
- `Generative AI` vs `human creativity`: novel-looking combinations do not imply human experience or human intent.
- `prompt` vs `input`: a prompt is one kind of input; the page also allows “other input.”
- `prompt` vs `brief`: the analogy uses a brief as a prompt-like request, but the terms need not be universal synonyms.
- `source material` vs `generated result`: source material is provided to the system; the generated result is produced by it.
- `representation` vs `generated output`: representation is an internal numerical form; output is the externally produced result.
- `tokens`, `pixels`, and `audio units`: these are different modality-specific output pieces, not interchangeable units.
- `generation` vs `review`: generation produces the result; review checks quality, safety, and fit afterward.
- `stopping condition` vs `review`: the stopping condition ends generation; review evaluates the completed output.
- `model` vs `system`: the model is the learned component; the page allows a user or surrounding system to provide instructions and verify results.
- `generative model` vs `Generative AI`: a generative model is the model component; Generative AI describes the broader capability/category.
- `structured content` vs `Structured Outputs`: structured content is a general output type mentioned in the definition; Structured Outputs is a linked concept and should not be collapsed automatically.
- `Deep Learning` vs `Generative AI`: Deep Learning describes a common way a system is built; Generative AI describes what the system does.
- `LLM` vs `Generative AI`: LLM is one related model category; Generative AI is the broader content-generating category in this page's framing.
- `image generation` vs `document extraction`: both are examples, but one produces visual content while the other maps document content into structured fields.
- `draft` vs `final answer`: the analogy explicitly says the generated result still needs checking, so a draft should not be treated as automatically final.

## Notes

- The page is intentionally beginner-friendly and gives an operational definition rather than a mathematical one.
- The source explicitly names modalities: text, images, audio, video, code, and structured content. Keep each modality as an independent raw candidate.
- The process is explicitly ordered as Prompt → Representation → Generation → Repeat → Review. The numbered labels and the unnumbered concept names are both retained.
- The page describes generation as incremental, but it does not formally define autoregressive decoding, probability, sampling, or tokenization; those belong in Potential Missing Concepts.
- `quality`, `safety`, and `fit` are review dimensions, not formal numeric metrics on this page. No concrete metric is provided in the source.
- The drafting, image-generation, and document-extraction examples each contain distinct Input / System / Output roles; the candidate inventory keeps those role labels and their concrete values.
- The comparison section uses `≠` statements as conceptual boundaries. These are retained as candidates for later normalization and taxonomy work.
- The related-concept links are source-grounded navigation terms, while the Potential Missing Concepts section is intentionally speculative and not evidence that the source page teaches those concepts.
- Video metadata (`Azure Neural voice`, `MP4`, `H.264`, `AAC`, and captions) is included because it appears in the complete page body, but it is peripheral to the core Generative AI explanation.
