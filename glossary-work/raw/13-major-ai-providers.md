## Notes

- The source page is intentionally an orientation page, not a company encyclopedia; preserve the high-level map and avoid treating the profiles as exhaustive provider histories.
- The 12 profile cards repeat the same generic fields and wording: `MODEL FAMILY`, `Who they are`, `Products`, `Known for`, and `Trade-off`. Repeated labels and repeated generic claims are retained as raw candidates.
- The provider map names xAI/Grok, Zhipu AI/GLM, MiniMax, ByteDance/Doubao, and Moonshot AI/Kimi, while the quick-comparison table only gives rows for OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Alibaba, and DeepSeek. This difference is retained as page evidence rather than normalized away.
- The comparison values are page-local orientation values, not durable claims about current products, access, licensing, modalities, or local execution. The profile trade-off explicitly says names, access, and licenses change.
- `Claude` is the clearest example of a brand crossing layers: the relation map says it is a model family plus branded product. Similar provider/model/product ambiguity should be reviewed for GPT, Gemini, Qwen, Grok, Llama, Mistral, GLM, Doubao, and Kimi.
- The page uses `open-weight`, not `open-source`, so those concepts should not be merged during later glossary cleanup.
- The table uses `Selected` for some open-weight values and `Partners` for Meta’s API value; these are preserved as literal comparison candidates because the page does not define their exact operational meaning.
- The page contains no quantitative performance metrics. `Open-weight`, `Local potential`, `Modalities`, and `Coding` are comparison dimensions/labels rather than measured benchmark metrics.
- Official links are preserved as first-party reference candidates. They are links from the source page, not independently verified facts about current provider offerings.
- Related pages are preserved because they provide the intended conceptual neighborhood: AI Model Landscape overview, Model Types & Families, and Open vs Closed / Local Models.
