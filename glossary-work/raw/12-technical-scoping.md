## Do Not Confuse Candidates

- **Technical scoping vs solution design**：Technical scoping defines the problem and feasible boundaries；solution design specifies the detailed implementation.
- **Technical scoping vs project plan**：Technical scoping clarifies what should be built and tested；a project plan schedules people, work, and delivery milestones.
- **Technical scoping vs proof of feasibility**：Technical scoping states assumptions and risks；proof of feasibility uses evidence to test those assumptions.
- **Technical scope vs system boundary**：Technical scope includes the problem, workflow, evidence, and decision context；system boundary is the specific line around what the system includes.
- **Business need vs outcome**：Business need motivates the work；outcome is the result the user or organization needs.
- **Outcome vs output**：Outcome is the desired effect or result；output is what the system produces to support that result.
- **Workflow vs system**：Workflow is the process of work；system is the bounded technical combination of models, tools, data, and integrations supporting it.
- **Workflow discovery vs technical scoping**：Discovery maps the current process；scoping chooses the feasible technical boundary based on that understanding.
- **Current process vs proposed AI workflow**：Current process describes how work happens now；proposed AI workflow describes a possible future system-supported flow.
- **Decision vs decision stage**：A decision is a point of choice in a workflow；Decision is the fifth named stage where the next step is chosen.
- **Handoff vs human fallback**：A handoff transfers responsibility；a human fallback is the specific alternative path used when AI handling is uncertain or fails.
- **Failure point vs risk**：A failure point is a location in a workflow where failure may occur；risk is the broader possibility and impact of a problem.
- **Assumption vs fact**：An assumption is a belief that may need evidence；a fact is an established condition. The page asks scoping to expose assumptions, not treat them as proof.
- **Evidence vs proof of feasibility**：Evidence is information used in evaluation；proof of feasibility is the stronger result of using evidence to test assumptions.
- **Test vs metric**：A test is a planned check or case；a metric is the measure used to judge results across cases or conditions.
- **Metric vs success condition**：A metric measures performance；a success condition states what must be true for the outcome to count as successful.
- **Review vs evaluation**：Review is an inspection step；evaluation is the broader process of testing and judging the workflow.
- **Prototype vs production system**：A prototype is a limited implementation for learning or testing；a production system is intended for operational use.
- **Build vs deploy**：Build creates or implements the system；deployment releases or operates it after the relevant evaluation.
- **Investigate vs prototype**：Investigate resolves unknowns through discovery; prototype tests a bounded implementation.
- **Stop vs failure**：Stop is a decision not to proceed; it does not necessarily mean the scoping process itself failed.
- **Input vs data**：Input is what enters this workflow instance；data is the broader information resource or technical component.
- **Output vs structured data**：Output may be any result or action；structured data is a particular machine-readable output format.
- **Policy vs rule**：Policy is domain guidance or governance information；rule is a condition used to validate a field or case.
- **Exception vs failure point**：Exception is a case requiring special handling；failure point is a location in the process where breakdown can occur.
- **Support triage vs document review**：Support triage classifies tickets and retrieves policy; document review extracts fields, validates rules, and flags exceptions.
- **Classify requests vs route incoming tickets**：Classification assigns a category; routing sends a request to the appropriate path, which may use that classification.
- **Human fallback vs human review**：Fallback is an alternative path when automation is uncertain; review is the inspection activity performed by a person or process.
- **Route vs workflow**：A route is the analogy’s path to a destination; a workflow is the real sequence of business work.
- **Destination vs success condition**：Destination is the endpoint in the travel analogy; success condition is the criterion used to judge an AI workflow.
- **Hazard vs risk**：Hazard is the analogy’s danger on a route; risk is the technical or business possibility represented by that danger.
- **Related concept vs source-backed definition**：A linked or neighboring concept is not necessarily explained in detail on this page.
- **Video metadata vs topic mechanism**：The video filename, poster, and captions are page metadata, not definitions of technical scoping.
